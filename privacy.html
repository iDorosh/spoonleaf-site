YOUR COOKBOOK. YOUR BUSINESS.

A little clarity
on privacy.

Spoonleaf gives your recipes a home.
Here’s where your information lives, what leaves your device, and what you control.

Privacy policy · Effective November 12, 2026

The short version

Spoonleaf does not require a separate account and does not include advertising or third-party analytics and tracking SDKs. Your cookbook is stored on your device and, when enabled and available, in your private iCloud storage. Importing a recipe contacts its website and image host. If you email for help, the information you send becomes part of that conversation.

1. Who this policy covers

This policy covers the Spoonleaf iPhone and iPad app and this website, provided by Ian Dorosh (“Spoonleaf,” “I,” or “we”). For privacy questions or requests, email ian.dorosh@outlook.com.

2. Your recipes and app preferences

Spoonleaf stores the cookbook content you choose to keep: recipe titles, ingredients, instructions, photos, source links, authors, notes, collections, favorites, recipe sizes, hidden-section preferences, and cooking history. Your device also stores app preferences, such as your chosen library layout and whether the screen stays awake while cooking.

Recipe extraction and editing happen on your device. Spoonleaf does not upload your cookbook to a separate server operated by us, and there is no Spoonleaf registration or login.

3. Private iCloud storage

When iCloud is enabled for Spoonleaf and available on your device, Apple’s CloudKit service can sync your cookbook, including downloaded photos and notes, through a private database associated with your Apple account. This uses your iCloud storage allowance and is subject to your Apple account and device settings. Local-only versions of Spoonleaf do not perform this sync.

Spoonleaf does not ask for or receive your Apple account password. Apple operates iCloud and handles its service data under Apple’s Privacy Policy. Apple’s CloudKit documentation explains how private data is stored in a user’s iCloud account.

Syncing is asynchronous. Account changes or disabling iCloud can affect which recipes are available on your device. Export a cookbook backup before changing accounts if you want to keep an independent copy.

4. Importing a recipe from the web

When you request an import, Spoonleaf contacts the URL you provide and, if available, the recipe’s image URL. Those websites and their hosting providers receive normal network-request information, such as your IP address, the requested URL, and request headers. Redirects may contact another host. Their own privacy practices apply.

The app parses the downloaded page on your device. It does not run the page’s advertising scripts or send the page to an AI extraction service. The importer uses temporary network sessions rather than reading Safari’s cookies or browsing history.

If you open a recipe’s original source in a browser, that visit is governed by the website’s and browser’s privacy settings.

5. Sharing from Safari and making backups

When you use Safari’s Share menu, the Spoonleaf extension receives the content you choose to share. After you review and confirm a recipe, the extension places it in a local shared inbox that Spoonleaf can access. The app transfers the recipe into your cookbook when it opens or becomes active. Successfully processed inbox files are removed; files that could not be processed are kept for retry.

Exporting a cookbook creates a backup file containing its recipe content and photos. You choose where to save or share that file. The file is not encrypted by Spoonleaf, so anyone with access to it can read its contents. A storage or sharing service you choose may process it under that service’s privacy policy.

6. Ads, tracking, and diagnostics

Spoonleaf does not display ads, sell your personal information, or use third-party analytics or advertising-tracking SDKs. The app does not request access to an advertising identifier.

Apple may process service, usage, or diagnostic information through iCloud, the App Store, or your device’s diagnostics features, according to your settings and Apple’s policies. This is separate from Spoonleaf’s recipe storage.

7. When you contact us

If you email ian.dorosh@outlook.com, we receive your email address and anything you include, such as your name, a message, or screenshots. We use that information to reply, troubleshoot, and handle follow-up. Email is processed by the email providers involved, including Microsoft Outlook for this contact address.

Support correspondence is kept as needed for those purposes or to meet legal obligations. You can ask us to access, correct, or delete information you sent. Please avoid including sensitive information or your full cookbook unless it is necessary for your request.

8. Visiting this website

This website does not add analytics scripts, advertising pixels, cookies, or browser-storage tracking. Its images, styles, and scripts are served with the site; it does not load third-party fonts or embeds.

The website is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub states that it logs visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes, including when a visitor is not signed in. See GitHub Pages’ data collection information and the GitHub Privacy Statement. Following an external link or an email link takes you to the relevant provider.

9. Your choices and deletion

  • Edit or remove content: change a recipe in the app, or move it to Recently Deleted. Items in Recently Deleted remain stored until you permanently delete them.
  • Keep a copy: use Export cookbook in Settings. Backup files you create remain wherever you saved them until you remove them there.
  • Manage iCloud: use your device’s Apple account and iCloud settings. Removing the app from a device does not necessarily remove data already in iCloud or in your backups.
  • Contact us: email to ask about information you have sent directly. Because we do not operate a separate cookbook server, we cannot retrieve or edit your private cookbook for you.

Depending on where you live, you may have additional rights to access, correct, erase, restrict, or object to processing of your personal information, and to contact your local privacy authority. Send requests concerning information held by us to the contact address above.

10. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as Spoonleaf develops. The current policy will remain available here, with its effective date updated when it changes. For questions about this policy or the app, send Ian an email.
